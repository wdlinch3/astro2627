Advanced Astronomy · Learning objectives

Celestial Navigation — Part I

Use these objectives to guide your work on this packet and its related lessons. Some objectives recur across packets. Test scope is specified separately.

I am able to…

  • explain position on Earth in terms of latitude and longitude.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Celestial coordinates
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.coordinates.010

  • explain the horizontal coordinate system using altitude and azimuth, identify the horizon and zenith, and state the reference direction and sense in which azimuth is measured.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Celestial coordinates
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.coordinates.020

  • locate specific objects in the sky using an appropriate coordinate system.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Celestial coordinates
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.coordinates.030

  • convert geographic coordinates between decimal and degrees–arcminutes–arcseconds form, and calculate surface distances from central angles along great-circle arcs on a spherical Earth.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Celestial coordinates
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.coordinates.070

  • construct and use a simple altitude-measuring instrument, estimate and justify its angular uncertainty, and explain how its design could be improved.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Observation and instruments
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.observations.010

  • record an object’s altitude and azimuth with the observing location and date/time, and use a chart or sky map for the same observing conditions to support an identification.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Observation and instruments
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.observations.020

  • compare coordinate estimates or measurements with a reference and assess whether their differences are consistent with the claimed uncertainty.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Observation and instruments
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.observations.030

  • distinguish a constellation from an asterism and use recognizable star patterns and coordinate grids to locate objects, including Polaris and the neighborhood of the equatorial coordinate origin.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Star charts and constellations
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.star_charts.010

  • calibrate the angular sizes of my own finger, fist, and outstretched hand from measured widths and eye-to-hand distances, and explain their use for rough observation.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Astranatomy
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.astranatomy.010

  • estimate angular sizes of objects and angular distances on the celestial sphere.

    Objective reference

    Celestial Sphere, Prominent Stars, Constellations · Astranatomy
    aa.ss.celestial_sphere.astranatomy.020
